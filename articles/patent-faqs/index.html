PATENT FAQs

By Dr Ritchie Daniel, Chartered Patent Attorney and European Patent Attorney. Last reviewed: November 2026.

Short answers to questions I regularly discuss with engineering businesses, startups and overseas clients. These explanations are general guidance, not advice on a particular invention or filing deadline.

For technical detail or a decision about a specific product, start with a high-level enquiry so that we can establish the scope and complete the necessary checks.

Deciding what to protect

What makes an engineering invention patentable?

A useful technical improvement may qualify, but a commercial advantage alone is not enough. Novelty and inventive step are important, alongside the other legal requirements. The question is what technical features distinguish the proposed invention from what was already publicly available. A patent attorney can help identify the contribution before committing to drafting.

Do I need a working prototype before filing a patent?

Not necessarily. The application must explain the invention in sufficient detail for a skilled person to carry out the claimed subject matter. Drawings, calculations, test results or a prototype may help, but the right point to file depends on how far the technical solution has developed. See when a startup should file.

Should a patent search be carried out before drafting?

A targeted search can help assess novelty, identify close prior art and guide claim strategy. The appropriate depth depends on the commercial decision and the available budget. No search can conclusively establish that an invention is patentable or that a product is free from infringement risk. Patentability and freedom to operate are different questions.

When should we file if the product is still changing?

The first filing needs enough technical substance to support the protection sought. Filing too early risks leaving important later developments outside the original disclosure. Waiting too long creates risks where publication, demonstrations or competitors are involved. A staged approach may make sense where separate inventions mature at different times. Read the filing-timing guide.

Can we show the invention to investors, suppliers or manufacturers before filing?

Public disclosure before filing can seriously affect patent rights, particularly outside jurisdictions with limited grace periods. Confidentiality agreements may help, but their effectiveness depends on the circumstances and how information is shared. Discuss planned demonstrations, pitch decks, trials and manufacturing discussions before any disclosure that could be problematic.

Preparing and drafting an application

What happens during the first invention discussion?

After initial scoping and conflict checks, I ask the engineers to describe the problem, their solution and the alternatives they explored. We also discuss competing approaches, prior art if known, commercial aims and anticipated changes to the product. The aim is to establish what is worth protecting before deciding how to claim the invention.

What should we prepare for a patent drafting meeting?

A concise explanation of the problem, sketches or drawings, a description of how the solution works and the known alternatives are a useful starting point. Relevant competing products, likely markets and planned disclosures also help. Please send only a high-level description at the enquiry stage. Confidential drawings and detailed technical material should follow once conflicts and engagement arrangements have been addressed.

What should good patent claims achieve?

Claims should identify the technical features that define the proposed protection. Clear language matters: the inventor should understand the intended scope, while the drafting must be supported by the application and account for relevant prior art. I usually test whether each claimed feature is essential and whether workable alternatives have been described.

Why does a patent attorney ask about alternative designs?

One version of a product may not be the only way to achieve the underlying technical result. Exploring different arrangements can reveal the real inventive principle and provide useful fall-back positions. The application must still describe enough detail to support any broader claims. Asking those questions early can be more valuable than trying to widen an application later.

Can one product lead to several patent applications?

Yes. A product can contain distinct inventions in its mechanisms, manufacturing methods, controls or other technical features. Sometimes related features belong in one application; sometimes a coordinated set of filings is more appropriate. Decisions depend on the inventions, unity requirements, timing, prior art, commercial priorities and cost. See RIDAN’s drafting approach.

How much does professional patent drafting cost?

The fee depends on the complexity of the invention, quality of the starting material, number of embodiments and scope of the work. Professional drafting is only one element of the overall filing and prosecution budget. Where the scope is reasonably clear, RIDAN IP can agree a fixed fee. See the UK patent application cost guide.

Can we use AI to prepare a patent application?

AI tools can help organise technical information and prepare working material, but inventors and advisers still need to check technical accuracy, claim support and disclosure quality. Confidentiality and data-handling arrangements also matter. An AI-generated draft is not a substitute for deciding what is actually inventive. Read the detailed discussion.

UK, European and international filing

Can a US or overseas company instruct a UK patent attorney directly?

Yes. Overseas businesses can instruct RIDAN IP directly for suitable UK and European patent matters, including original drafting, filing and prosecution. We first agree the technical fit, scope, filing route and responsibilities. A US business does not necessarily need a separate US firm in order to instruct a UK patent attorney for UK work. Read more.

Does a PCT application automatically become a UK patent?

No. A PCT application is an international filing route, not an international patent grant. To pursue UK protection, the applicant must take the required steps for UK national-phase entry within the applicable deadline. For many applications the normal deadline is 31 months from the relevant priority date, but timing and requirements must be checked for each case. UK national-phase support is available.

Should we apply for a UK patent, a European patent or both?

The right route depends on where protection matters, the commercial plan, existing applications and the budget. A European application can cover a wider range of countries, subject to grant and the steps needed for protection in individual states. UK and European routes can interact, so the filing strategy needs to be planned rather than simply duplicated.

Can RIDAN act as a UK or European associate for my patent firm?

Yes. RIDAN IP works with overseas patent attorneys and agents on direct filings, PCT phase entry, prosecution and suitable original drafting instructions. The relationship with your client, reporting arrangements, billing and communication channels are agreed in advance. See support for overseas attorneys.

What happens after the application is filed?

Patent offices commonly conduct searches and substantive examination. The applicant may need to respond to objections, amend claims and make decisions about the scope of protection. Examination, grant and subsequent maintenance involve further cost and deadlines. The process is not automatic, and a filing does not guarantee a granted patent.

Will you tell us when a patent is not worth filing?

Yes. An application should serve a commercial purpose. If the likely protection is too narrow, the invention is not ready, the likely cost is disproportionate or another form of protection would be better, I will explain the concerns and available options. The objective is a sound business decision, not filing for the sake of filing.

What is the difference between patentability and freedom to operate?

Patentability asks whether an invention may meet the requirements for patent protection. Freedom to operate considers whether making, using or selling a particular product may infringe another party’s rights in relevant countries. An invention can be patentable and still raise infringement risks. Explore clearance and IP opinions.

Who owns inventions developed by an external design consultancy?

Ownership depends on the contractual arrangements and the applicable law. Commissioning design work does not necessarily transfer every patent or other intellectual property right to the commissioner. Address ownership, confidentiality and rights to improvements in writing before development begins. Read the IP ownership guide.
