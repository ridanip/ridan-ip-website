MECHANICAL INVENTIONS

Why technical understanding matters.

In a mechanical invention, the useful patentable idea may lie in the relationship between components rather than in any one part. Geometry, movement, force paths, fluid flow, thermal behaviour, materials, manufacturing constraints and control can all affect how the invention should be described and claimed.

Ritchie's mechanical-engineering background allows inventors and engineering teams to discuss drawings, CAD, prototypes and product architecture directly with the attorney responsible for the work. The aim is to understand the technology first, then identify the broader inventive concept and the narrower technical positions that may matter during examination or later product development.

This is particularly useful where a product is still changing. A patent application fixes the technical disclosure available at the filing date, so the timing and content of the first application should be considered alongside development, disclosure and commercial plans.
